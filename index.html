Features

Built for real conversations

Everything runs on your own device, and nothing is uploaded.

Sign → Tulu

The webcam tracks 21 points on each hand and matches the sign against your recorded ISL clips. The result appears and is spoken in Tulu.

Speech → Text

Speech in Tulu, Kannada, English and nine other Indian languages appears as large, easy-to-read text.

Instant feedback

The whole movement is matched against your recordings, and a confidence meter confirms a sign only when it is clear, so you avoid false results.

Private by design

The camera and recognition run in the browser. No video ever leaves your computer.

How it works

From a sign to a spoken word in under a second

  1. 1

    Capture

    MediaPipe Hands finds up to two hands inside the frame on screen.

  2. 2

    Describe

    Each hand becomes 63 numbers that describe its shape, regardless of hand size or distance.

  3. 3

    Match

    The hand shape is compared with the average of each sign in your dataset, and the closest clear match wins.

  4. 4

    Speak

    The sign is shown in English and Tulu (Kannada script) and read aloud.

    About

    About SilentTalk

    Two-way communication using Indian Sign Language recognition and Tulu language translation.

    Why it exists

    Tulu is spoken by nearly two million people in coastal Karnataka and northern Kerala, yet there are almost no tools that connect it with sign language. When a deaf or mute person and a Tulu speaker meet, they often have to rely on gestures or written notes.

    SilentTalk closes that gap with a camera and a microphone. Signs become Tulu words the other person can hear and read, and speech becomes text the signer can read.
